Getting started

Recruiting has changed more in the past ten years than in the previous forty. The best recruiters become trusted advisors. Here is where that starts.

Start with your brand

Candidates judge the person before they judge the role. A strong profile earns trust before you speak, helps the right people self-select, and opens warmer conversations.

1. Craft a killer opening

Over-invest in your headline and summary. Write like you want to hire.

2. Add rich media

Show your work with images, video and presentations.

3. Add certifications

Credentials build credibility fast.

4. Show volunteer work

Paint a fuller picture of who you are.

5. Make updates interactive

Mention people and companies. Reply when they comment.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Expand any question for the answer.

1What’s in it for me?

Your daily work gets easier, because these skills fix what slows you down now: roles that change after weeks of sourcing, shortlists rejected without a reason, candidates who go quiet, and decisions you cannot back with evidence. People start treating you as an advisor, not just a recruiter. And you earn certificates that go on your profile and stay with you for good.

2I already use AI tools every day. Do I need to learn “Responsible AI and recruiting technology fluency”?

Using AI tools is not the same as being fluent in them. The gap the framework identifies is knowing where AI accelerates work, where human judgment is essential, and how to manage bias, privacy, transparency and data-quality risks. Most daily use never teaches the third part.

3Where do I honestly start?

Where you are facing challenges during requisitions:

Searches dying at assessment

Start with skills-based hiring

Hiring managers reopening settled decisions

Start with talent advisory

Candidates going quiet after outreach

Start with candidate engagement

Being asked to forecast rather than fill

Start with talent intelligence
4I’m not a numbers person. Can I skip learning “Talent intelligence, analytics and workforce planning”?

It asks you to interpret data instead of creating it. It turns market, funnel and internal-skills data into a view of supply, demand and risk. If you have ever told a hiring manager a role will be hard to fill, you have already done a version of this.

5What does being a ‘talent advisor’ actually change about my day?

Mostly, it changes when you get involved. Today a role lands in your queue already decided, and your first move is to start sourcing. As an advisor, your first move is a conversation: what problem is this hire solving, which of these requirements are real, and does the market actually contain this person at this salary. Some of those roles get reshaped in that conversation. A few get withdrawn. The rest you start sourcing with a brief that holds.

The visible difference is what stops happening. Fewer weeks spent on a role that gets rewritten. Fewer shortlists rejected on criteria nobody mentioned at kickoff. Fewer searches quietly abandoned after two months. You are doing less work, on better-defined roles, with hiring managers who already agree what good looks like.

6Will my manager sign off on the time?

Lead with the impact instead of the course list. Each skill names what it moves: cycle time and capacity, workforce decisions and time-to-fill, pool width and role fit, alignment and stalled searches, response and acceptance rates. Pick the one matching the metric your team is currently missing.

7If I only do one thing, what should it be?

Take one skill, one live requisition, and apply the learning to it this month. The framework is built to be used against real work, not completed as a course list.
